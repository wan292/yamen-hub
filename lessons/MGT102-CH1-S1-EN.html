Chapter 1 — What Is Management?

MGT 102 · Chapter 1 · Section 1 of 4: What Is Management?
Objective LO1-1: Describe what management is, why management is important, what managers do, and how managers use organizational resources efficiently and effectively to achieve organizational goals.

The first lesson of the course — and the base of everything after it

The whole of MGT 102 turns on one question: what does a manager do so the organization reaches its goals? The book's answer is the definition you will memorize here: managers use resources to achieve organizational goals efficiently and effectively. This lesson gives you the words you will see in every question after it: organization, manager, management, efficiency, effectiveness.

Chapter 1 has four sections: 1 What Is Management? · 2 The Four Functions of Management · 3 Levels and Skills of Managers · 4 Recent Changes and Global Challenges. Start here, and memorize the definitions in English, exactly as they are — the exam is in English, and the wrong choices change only one word of the definition.

You already met these ideas in Arabic. This page gives you the same ideas in English, because your textbook and your exam are in English.

How to use this page: tap or hover any underlined word to see the Arabic. Words with a solid line are vocabulary to learn — they show a translation plus a short explanation, and they are collected in the table at the end. Words with a dotted line just give you a quick translation if you get stuck. The green EXAM BOX text has no help on purpose — read it the way it will appear on the exam.

Organizations and Managers
The two words every question in this course is built on
THE IDEA IN ONE LINE
An organization = people working together for a goal. A manager = the person responsible for making sure the organization's resources are used well to reach that goal.
🧠
Picture a football team: players = the organization, coach = the manager. He does not play, but he makes sure everyone plays right to win.
EXPLANATION

The book starts with a question: when you hear the word "manager", who comes to mind? It gives three different pictures:

  • A top executive like Brian Chesky, the chief executive officer (CEO) of Airbnb.
  • A fast-food restaurant manager who deals directly with employees and customers.
  • A supervisor at a manufacturing company.

All three are managers, no matter how different their jobs look. The book says they share two characteristics:

#Shared characteristicWhat it means
1 They all work inside organizations An organization = a group of people who work together and coordinate their actions to achieve a wide variety of goals, or "desired future outcomes".
2 They are all responsible for the resources A manager = the person responsible for supervising the use of the organization's human and other resources, and making the most of them, to achieve its goals.

Note: for the book, an organization is people, not a building, a logo, or a business license. A restaurant, a hospital, a club, a university, a global company — all of them are organizations because the people in them coordinate their work toward a shared goal.

THE BOOK'S EXAMPLE — A MANAGER'S CHALLENGE: AIRBNB

The book opens the chapter with the story of Brian Chesky. When COVID-19 came, Airbnb's revenues dived by 80% in the first two months — he calls it a "near-death business experience" for the company. What did he do?

  • He reviewed the company's structure to make it more efficient without losing its capacity to innovate: he cut 10 departments down to a few basic functions, put new initiatives on hold, slashed the marketing budget, and laid off 1,900 people.
  • He focused on innovation: upgrades to the service twice a year (for example, search by a traveler's interests, not only by location).
  • He monitored the rapidly changing business environment: travel patterns changed, and he noticed that remote employees were more productive. So he tested working from different Airbnb locations himself, then set a "work from anywhere" policy, with one week in the office each quarter to build trust.
  • He cared about the community: temporary housing for displaced people (Superstorm Sandy in 2012, refugees from Afghanistan and Ukraine).

The result: two years later, bookings and revenues rose above their previous highs. The book uses the story to answer one question: how do managers contribute to their organization's success? — through decisions about resources (people, money, structure) in an environment that changes fast.

EXAM BOX

Organizations are collections of people who work together and coordinate their actions to achieve a wide variety of goals or desired future outcomes. A manager is a person responsible for supervising the use of an organization's resources to meet its goals. Managers are the people responsible for supervising and making the most of an organization's human and other resources to achieve its goals.

TRAP
  • "Organization" = a group of people who coordinate their actions — any choice that defines it as a building, capital, or a product is wrong.
  • A manager is responsible for human and other resources — not only people. A choice that says "supervises employees only" is incomplete.
  • A manager is not only the CEO: the restaurant manager and the factory supervisor are managers too. A choice that says "only executives are managers" is wrong.

Management and Organizational Resources
A definition with three pieces — the exam swaps one piece and keeps the rest
THE IDEA IN ONE LINE
Management = four actions (planning, organizing, leading, controlling) that you do on resources to reach goals efficiently and effectively.
🧠
Picture a restaurant: cooks, oven, meat, cash, loyal customers — all "resources". Management is planning, organizing, leading and controlling them so the place makes money.
EXPLANATION

The book's definition of management has three pieces. Learn them in order, because the exam changes one piece and keeps the rest:

PieceIn EnglishWhat it means
1 · What do you do? planning, organizing, leading, and controlling Planning, organizing, leading, controlling — four functions, in this order. Section 2 is all about them.
2 · On what? of human and other resources People, and everything else the organization has.
3 · Why? to achieve organizational goals efficiently and effectively To reach the organization's goals with efficiency and effectiveness — both words, always together.

What are resources? The book lists them by name, and each one can appear as a choice in a question like "which of the following is NOT a resource?":

  • people and their skills, know-how, and experience
  • machinery
  • raw materials
  • computers and information technology
  • patents
  • financial capital
  • loyal customers and employees — yes, a regular customer is a "resource"!
EXPLANATION — A MANAGER'S GOAL: HIGH PERFORMANCE

One of the key goals of any organization: to provide goods and services that customers value and desire. The book gives three examples of the same idea:

ManagerPrincipal goal
Brian Chesky at Airbnb Meet travelers' and remote workers' needs for a place to sleep, work, relax, or seek out adventure.
A fast-food manager Tasty and convenient food that customers enjoy and come back to buy.
A manufacturing manager Balance the quality customers need against the pressure to be cost effective.

Notice that every manager's goal is to satisfy the customer in some way — and that leads us to the big question of the next concept: how do we measure whether the manager succeeded?

EXAM BOX

Management is the planning, organizing, leading, and controlling of human and other resources to achieve organizational goals efficiently and effectively. An organization's resources include assets such as people and their skills, know-how, and experience; machinery; raw materials; computers and information technology; and patents, financial capital, and loyal customers and employees. One of the key goals that organizations try to achieve is to provide goods and services that customers value and desire.

TRAP
  • The number is four: planning, organizing, leading, controlling. Any choice that puts marketing, budgeting, or selling in their place is wrong.
  • The definition ends with efficiently and effectively — both. A choice that says only "efficiently", or "quickly and cheaply", is wrong.
  • The question "which of the following is NOT an organizational resource": loyal customers, patents, and employees' know-how are all resources. Do not cross them out.
  • Management is a process (actions), not a job title and not a department. "Management is the top floor of the company" = wrong.

Efficiency, Effectiveness, and Performance
The most important concept in the section — the exam always tests the difference
THE IDEA IN ONE LINE
Efficiency = doing the thing with fewer resources and less time. Effectiveness = choosing the right goal and reaching it. Performance goes up when both go up together.
🧠
Picture a burger place: cheapest and fastest burgers in town (high efficiency)... but the whole neighborhood is vegetarian (zero effectiveness). Great work, wrong goal.
EXPLANATION

The book starts with the measure of success: Organizational performance = a measure of how efficiently and effectively managers use the available resources to satisfy customers and achieve the organization's goals. Performance increases in direct proportion to increases in efficiency and effectiveness. So what is the difference between the two?

Efficiency · الكفاءةEffectiveness · الفعالية
Definition A measure of how productively resources are used to achieve a goal. A measure of the appropriateness of the goals that managers have selected for the organization to pursue, and the degree to which they are achieved.
The question it answers How do we do the thing? — "Did we do the thing right?" What do we do? — "Did we do the right thing?"
When is an organization like this? When managers minimize the input resources (labor, raw materials, component parts) or the time needed to produce a given output of goods or services. Organizations are effective when managers choose appropriate goals and then achieve them.
The book's examples Wendy's: fryers that use less oil and cook faster.
UPS: drivers leave the truck door open on short trips to save delivery time.
Momentive (formerly SurveyMonkey): CEO Zander Lurie retooled products, eliminated an unsuccessful business, and expanded successful ones.		 McDonald's: they chose the goal of offering breakfast to attract more customers, then made it all day — the right goal, and it worked.

High-performing companies like Apple, Amazon, Walmart, Home Depot, Accenture, and Habitat for Humanity are both at the same time (simultaneously efficient and effective). And an effective manager = one who chooses the right goals and has the skills to utilize resources efficiently.

Figure 1.1 — Efficiency, Effectiveness, and Performance in an Organization

This is the book's own layout. The vertical axis is Efficiency (top = High, bottom = Low) and the horizontal axis is Effectiveness (left = Low, right = High). Each box says: what the manager chose (right / wrong goal) + how he used the resources (good / poor) = what the customer gets. The top-right box (High / High) is the high-performing organization. If a question says "the manager chose the right goal but wasted resources" = the bottom-right box: a product the customer wants but too expensive for him. And "used resources well but the goal was wrong" = the top-left box: a high-quality product that nobody wants.

EXAM BOX

Organizational performance is a measure of how efficiently and effectively managers use available resources to satisfy customers and achieve organizational goals. Efficiency is a measure of how productively resources are used to achieve a goal. Organizations are efficient when managers minimize the amount of input resources (such as labor, raw materials, and component parts) or the amount of time needed to produce a given output of goods or services. Effectiveness is a measure of the appropriateness of the goals that managers have selected for the organization to pursue and the degree to which the organization achieves those goals. Organizations are effective when managers choose appropriate goals and then achieve them. High-performing organizations are simultaneously efficient and effective.

TRAP
  • The most famous trap in the chapter: they give you the definition of efficiency and write "effectiveness" under it, or the reverse. Efficiency = resources and time (how). Effectiveness = appropriate goals (what).
  • A scenario with "less oil / faster / lower cost / fewer workers" = efficiency. A scenario with "they chose a goal / a new service and customers loved it" = effectiveness.
  • Performance rises with both. A choice that says "performance depends on efficiency only" is wrong.
  • In Figure 1.1: high efficiency + low effectiveness = a high-quality product customers do not want (not "an expensive product"). High effectiveness + low efficiency = a product they want but too expensive. Do not flip them.

Why Study Management?
Four reasons, two numbers, and one term (social learning) the exam likes
THE IDEA IN ONE LINE
Four reasons: managerial skills are in demand, you learn from other people's mistakes, the pay is good, and it helps you outside work too.
🧠
Picture a thousand managers who made mistakes before you and wrote them in one book. Studying management = reading that book instead of repeating the mistakes.
EXPLANATION

The book gives four reasons. The exam usually asks about the numbers or about the term social learning:

#ReasonDetails from the book
1 Managerial skills are in demand Modern work is dynamic and complex, so managerial skills are in demand. Organizations need people who can understand this complexity, respond to environmental contingencies, and make decisions that are ethical and effective.
2 You learn from other people's experience
social learning		 We learn through personal experience or the experiences of others. Studying management exposes you to the lessons others have learned, so you are less likely to repeat their mistakes. And by studying and practicing the behaviors of good managers and high-performing companies, you equip yourself to help your future employer succeed.
3 The economic benefits In the United States, a general manager earns a median wage of $97,970, with a projected growth in job openings of between 4% and 7% until 2031.
4 Better decisions outside work Coaching a child's baseball team, organizing a charity 5K run, planning your budget, starting a new business — management principles help you understand others, make quality decisions, and improve your personal success.
EXAM BOX

Organizations need people who can understand this complexity, respond to environmental contingencies, and make decisions that are ethical and effective. By studying management in school, you are exposing yourself to the lessons others have learned. The advantage of such social learning is that you are less likely to repeat the mistakes others have made in the past. In the United States, general managers earn a median wage of $97,970, with a projected growth in job openings of between 4% and 7% between now and 2031. Learning management principles can help you make good decisions in nonwork contexts.

TRAP
  • Social learning = you learn from other people's experiences, not "studying with friends in a group". The advantage: you do not repeat their mistakes.
  • The numbers: $97,970 median wage (median, not average), and 4%–7% growth until 2031. Any other number is wrong.
  • Management helps outside work too (sports, charity, your budget). A choice that says "management principles are useful only in companies" is wrong.

Test yourself

Answer first, then open the solution. If you miss question 3 or 4, go back to the efficiency / effectiveness table above.

1. A collection of people who work together and coordinate their actions to achieve a wide variety of goals or desired future outcomes is best described as:
  1. A department
  2. An organization
  3. A management team
  4. A resource
Answer

B. This is the book's exact definition of an organization. Choice C is a trap: the management team is only the managers, not all the people. Choice D mixes things up: people are one of the organization's resources, but the organization itself is the group that coordinates.

2. According to the textbook's definition of management, which of the following is NOT one of the four activities managers perform on human and other resources?
  1. Planning
  2. Organizing
  3. Marketing
  4. Controlling
Answer

C. The four are planning, organizing, leading, controlling. Marketing is an activity of one department in a company, not a function of management. The trap is putting a word that sounds "managerial" between three correct words.

3. Wendy's develops new fat fryers that use less oil and cook french fries faster. UPS instructs drivers to leave truck doors open on short trips to reduce delivery time. The textbook uses these examples to illustrate:
  1. Effectiveness
  2. Efficiency
  3. Organizational structure
  4. Social learning
Answer

B. Less oil and less time for the same output = fewer inputs = efficiency. Choice A is the swap trap: effectiveness is about choosing the right goal, and nothing in the scenario is about choosing a goal. Choice D is a word from another concept, put there to distract you.

4. According to Figure 1.1, a manager who chooses the right goals to pursue but makes poor use of resources to achieve these goals will most likely produce:
  1. A product that customers want at a quality and price they can afford
  2. A high-quality product that customers do not want
  3. A product that customers want, but that is too expensive for them to buy
  4. A low-quality product that customers do not want
Answer

C. Right goal = high effectiveness, and poor use of resources = low efficiency → the bottom-right box: the customer wants it but it is too expensive. A is the High / High box. B is the reversed box (high efficiency, low effectiveness) — the most famous trap. D is Low / Low.

5. Organizational performance is best defined as a measure of:
  1. The total number of employees and customers an organization has
  2. How efficiently and effectively managers use available resources to satisfy customers and achieve organizational goals
  3. The appropriateness of the goals that managers have selected for the organization to pursue
  4. How productively resources are used to achieve a goal
Answer

B. This is the book's definition. Choice C is the definition of effectiveness alone, and D is the definition of efficiency alone — the trap is giving you half of the definition as if it were the whole. Performance = both together.
