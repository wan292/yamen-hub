الشرح — من آخر الفصل: إطار Managing Ethically (القرارات المتطرّفة)

في Management in Action وتحت LO7-3، الكتاب يضيف ملاحظة عن المجموعات: أحياناً تطلّع قرارات متطرّفة (extreme decisions) — يعني قرارات أكثر مخاطرة أو أكثر تحفّظ من اللي كان كل واحد بيقرّره لو كان لحاله. وتفسير واحد لهالشي اسمه diffusion of responsibility (تشتّت المسؤولية): في المجموعة مسؤولية نتايج القرار تتوزّع على الأعضاء، فكل واحد يحسّ إنه مو مسؤول بالكامل، والقرار يطلع متطرّف لأن محد شال مسؤوليته كاملة.

والكتاب يسألك للنقاش: وش التبعات الأخلاقية للقرارات المتطرّفة في المجموعات؟ وهل المفروض كل عضو يحسّ إنه مسؤول بالكامل عن نتيجة القرار الجماعي؟ ما فيه جواب رسمي — بس انتبه إن هذا مو groupthink: groupthink = سعي للاتفاق، وdiffusion of responsibility = مسؤولية متوزّعة.

EXAM BOX

Many (or perhaps most) important organizational decisions are made by groups or teams of managers rather than by individuals. Group decision making is superior to individual decision making in several respects. When managers work as a team to make decisions and solve problems, their choices of alternatives are less likely to fall victim to the biases and errors discussed previously. They can draw on the combined skills, competencies, and accumulated knowledge of group members and thereby improve their ability to generate feasible alternatives and make good decisions. Group decision making also allows managers to process more information and to correct one another’s errors. And in the implementation phase, all managers affected by the decisions agree to cooperate. When a group of managers makes a decision (as opposed to one top manager making a decision and imposing it on subordinate managers), the probability that the decision will be implemented successfully increases.

Some potential disadvantages are associated with group decision making. Groups often take much longer than individuals to make decisions. Getting two or more managers to agree to the same solution can be difficult because managers’ interests and preferences are often different. In addition, just like decision making by individual managers, group decision making can be undermined by biases. A major source of group bias is groupthink.

EXAM BOX

Groupthink is a pattern of faulty and biased decision making that occurs in groups whose members strive for agreement among themselves at the expense of accurately assessing information relevant to a decision. When managers are subject to groupthink, they collectively embark on a course of action without developing appropriate criteria to evaluate alternatives. Typically, a group rallies around one central manager, such as the CEO, and the course of action that manager supports. Group members become blindly committed to that course of action without evaluating its merits. Commitment is often based on an emotional, rather than an objective, assessment of the optimal course of action.

The decision President Kennedy and his advisers made to launch the unfortunate Bay of Pigs invasion in Cuba in 1962, the decisions made by President Johnson and his advisers from 1964 to 1967 to escalate the war in Vietnam, the decision made by President Nixon and his advisers in 1972 to cover up the Watergate break-in, and the decision made by NASA and Morton Thiokol in 1986 to launch the ill-fated Challenger shuttle—all were likely influenced by groupthink. After the fact, decision makers such as these who may fall victim to groupthink are often surprised that their decision-making process and outcomes were so flawed.

When groupthink occurs, pressures for agreement and harmony within a group have the unintended effect of discouraging individuals from raising issues that run counter to majority opinion. For example, when managers at NASA and Morton Thiokol fell victim to groupthink, they convinced each other that all was well and that there was no need to delay the launch of the Challenger space shuttle.

One major source of poor decision making is groupthink. Afflicted decision makers collectively embark on a dubious course of action without questioning the assumptions that underlie their decision.

EXAM BOX

Two techniques known to counteract groupthink and cognitive biases are devil’s advocacy and dialectical inquiry (see Figure 7.7).

devil’s advocacy Critical analysis of a preferred alternative, made in response to challenges raised by a group member who, playing the role of devil’s advocate, defends unpopular or opposing alternatives for the sake of argument.

Devil’s advocacy is a critical analysis of a preferred alternative to ascertain its strengths and weaknesses before it is implemented. Typically, one member of the decision-making group plays the role of devil’s advocate. The devil’s advocate critiques and challenges the way the group evaluated alternatives and chose one over the others. The purpose of devil’s advocacy is to identify all the reasons that might make the preferred alternative unacceptable. In this way, decision makers can be made aware of the possible perils of recommended courses of action.

الفخ
  • Devil’s advocacy = بديل واحد (a preferred alternative) + عضو واحد (one member). لو السؤال قال «بديلين» أو «مجموعتين» → هذا dialectical inquiry.
  • الـdevil’s advocate يدافع عن البدائل غير الشعبية أو المعارضة for the sake of argument — مو عن البديل الشعبي، ومو لأنه بالضرورة مقتنع فيها.
  • الغرض = يطلّع كل الأسباب اللي تخلّي البديل المفضّل غير مقبول — مو يدوّر بديل جديد أحسن (هذا هدف dialectical inquiry).
  • التوقيت: before it is implemented — قبل التنفيذ مو بعده.
  • آخر خطوة في الشكل: Accept? Modify? Reject? — ثلاث احتمالات. كلمة Combine ما تجي هنا.

  4. خلال هالنقاش (debate) الإدارة العليا تتحدّى موقف المجموعتين عشان تكشف المشاكل والمخاطر المحتملة في حلولهم.
  5. الهدف: مسار عمل أحسن حتى من الاثنين (an even better alternative).

وملزمة الكلاس تكتب تعريفه كذا: a critical evaluation of two alternatives in which separate groups present their preferred courses of action and critique each other’s position — نفس الفكرة: بديلين + مجموعتين + كل وحدة تنتقد الثانية.

هذا هو Figure 7.7 في الكتاب (ص ٢٠٠). فيه عمودين جنب بعض. يسار الصورة — Devil’s Advocacy: ثلاث مربعات حمراء فوق بعض بأسهم لتحت: Present chosen alternative ← Critique chosen alternative ← Reassess chosen alternative (Accept? Modify? Reject?). يمين الصورة — Dialectical Inquiry: فوق مربعين أخضر جنب بعض (Present alternative #1 و Present alternative #2)، سهمينهم ينزلون ويلتقون في مربع أحمر واحد Debate between alternatives، وبعده مربع بنفسجي Reassess alternatives (Accept #1 or #2? Combine #1 and #2?). كيف تقرأه: عدّ مربعات الصف الأول — واحد يسار = بديل واحد، اثنين يمين = بديلين. واليسار كله chosen alternative بالمفرد، واليمين alternatives بالجمع. اليسار ٣ خطوات، واليمين ٤ مربعات. وش يجي منه بالاختبار: «أي خطوة تجي في dialectical inquiry بس؟» (Debate between alternatives أو Combine #1 and #2)، أو «وش ترتيب خطوات devil’s advocacy؟» (Present ← Critique ← Reassess)، أو يعطيك آخر خطوة ويسألك لأي تقنية: Accept? Modify? Reject? = devil’s advocacy، وAccept #1 or #2? Combine? = dialectical inquiry.

EXAM BOX

dialectical inquiry Critical analysis of two preferred alternatives in order to find an even better alternative for the organization to adopt.

Dialectical inquiry goes one step further. Two groups of managers are assigned to a problem, and each group is responsible for evaluating alternatives and selecting one of them. Top managers hear each group present its preferred alternative, and then each group critiques the other’s position. During this debate, top managers challenge both groups’ positions to uncover potential problems and perils associated with their solutions. The goal is to find an even better alternative course of action for the organization to adopt.

Both devil’s advocacy and dialectical inquiry can help counter the effects of cognitive biases and groupthink. In practice, devil’s advocacy is probably easier to implement because it involves less managerial time and effort than does dialectical inquiry.

الفخ
  • العدّ يحلّ أغلب الأسئلة: ١ بديل + ١ عضو = devil’s advocacy؛ ٢ بدائل + ٢ مجموعات = dialectical inquiry.
  • الأسهل تطبيقاً = devil’s advocacy (وقت وجهد إداري أقل). الاختيار اللي يقول «dialectical inquiry أسهل لأن الشغل متوزّع على مجموعتين» = غلط.
  • goes one step further = وصف dialectical inquiry، مو devil’s advocacy.
  • Debate between alternatives وCombine #1 and #2 = بس في dialectical inquiry. Modify = بس في devil’s advocacy.
  • اللي يتحدّى موقف المجموعتين = الإدارة العليا (top managers)، مو عضو واحد.
  • الاثنين علاج لـgroupthink والتحيّزات — مو أنواع من groupthink. والكتاب يقول probably easier (غالباً) — مو «دايماً».

EXAM BOX

Another way to improve group decision making is to promote diversity in decision-making groups (see Chapter 5). Bringing together managers from various ethnic, national, and functional backgrounds broadens the range of life experiences and opinions that group members can draw on as they generate, assess, and choose among alternatives. Moreover, diverse groups are sometimes less prone to groupthink because group members already differ from each other and thus are less subject to pressures for uniformity.

Managers can improve the quality of group decision making by using techniques such as devil’s advocacy and dialectical inquiry and by increasing diversity in the decision-making group.

الفخ
  • الكتاب يقول sometimes less prone — أحياناً أقل عرضة، مو «ما يصير فيها groupthink أبد».
  • السبب = الأعضاء أصلاً مختلفين ← أقل خضوع لضغوط التماثل (pressures for uniformity). مو لأن المجموعة المتنوّعة «تقرّر أسرع» — القرار الجماعي أصلاً يطوّل.
  • الخلفيات ثلاث: ethnic · national · functional. انتبه لكلمة functional (وظيفية/أقسام) — تنسى كثير.
  • طرق تحسين القرار الجماعي ثلاث: devil’s advocacy · dialectical inquiry · التنوّع. الاختيار اللي يقول «خلّ المجموعة تلتفّ حول الـCEO» أو «قلّل النقاش عشان تسرّع الاتفاق» = وصفة groupthink مو علاجه.

اختبر نفسك

الأسئلة بالإنجليزي لأن الاختبار بالإنجليزي. جاوب قبل ما تفتح الحل. لو غلطت في ١ ارجع لجدولي المزايا والعيوب في المفهوم الأول، ولو غلطت في ٣ أو ٤ ارجع لجدول «Devil’s advocacy ضد Dialectical inquiry» والشكل في المفهوم الرابع.

1. Which of the following is NOT an advantage of group decision making described in the chapter?
  1. Choices of alternatives are less likely to fall victim to biases and errors.
  2. Managers can process more information and correct one another’s errors.
  3. Groups usually make decisions more quickly than individuals.
  4. Managers affected by the decision agree to cooperate when it is implemented.
الجواب

C. الكتاب يقول العكس: Groups often take much longer than individuals to make decisions — هذا عيب رقم ١، والاختيار قلبه ميزة. A وB وD كلها من المزايا الأربع: أقل عرضة للتحيّزات والأخطاء، معلومات أكثر وتصحيح أخطاء بعض، والتعاون في مرحلة التنفيذ. والسؤال بـNOT، فلا تختار أول جملة صحيحة تشوفها.

2. The top management team of a retail chain rallies around the CEO’s plan to enter a new market. Members become blindly committed to the plan without developing criteria to evaluate alternatives, and pressures for harmony discourage anyone from raising concerns that run counter to the majority opinion. This situation is best described as:
  1. escalating commitment
  2. devil’s advocacy
  3. diffusion of responsibility
  4. groupthink
الجواب

D. كل كلمات groupthink موجودة: rallies around one central manager, such as the CEO، blindly committed، without developing appropriate criteria، وpressures for agreement and harmony تمنع اللي يعارض رأي الأغلبية. الفخ في A: escalating commitment = تضخ موارد إضافية في مشروع بان فشله — هنا ما فيه مشروع فاشل. وB هو العلاج مو المشكلة. وC من إطار Managing Ethically: مسؤولية متوزّعة تطلّع قرار متطرّف — مو التفاف حول الرئيس.

3. Top managers assign two groups of managers to the same problem. Each group evaluates alternatives and selects one, presents its preferred alternative, and critiques the other group’s position while top managers challenge both positions. Which technique is being used?
  1. Devil’s advocacy
  2. Dialectical inquiry
  3. Groupthink
  4. Satisficing
الجواب

B. مجموعتين + بديلين + كل مجموعة تنتقد الثانية + الإدارة العليا تتحدّى الاثنين = dialectical inquiry، وهدفه بديل أحسن حتى من الاثنين. الفخ في A: devil’s advocacy = عضو واحد ينتقد بديل واحد، وهو الأسهل تطبيقاً لأنه ياخذ وقت وجهد أقل. وC هو المشكلة اللي هالتقنيات تعالجها. وD: satisficing من درس ١ (تختار بديل مقبول من عيّنة محدودة) — ما له علاقة.

4. According to Figure 7.7, which sequence shows the steps of devil’s advocacy?
  1. Present alternative #1 and alternative #2 → Debate between alternatives → Reassess alternatives
  2. Critique chosen alternative → Present chosen alternative → Reassess chosen alternative
  3. Present chosen alternative → Critique chosen alternative → Reassess chosen alternative (Accept? Modify? Reject?)
  4. Present chosen alternative → Debate between alternatives → Combine #1 and #2
الجواب

C. هذا العمود اليسار في Figure 7.7 بالضبط: ثلاث مربعات عن بديل واحد (chosen alternative بالمفرد)، وآخرها Accept? Modify? Reject?. الفخ في A: هذا العمود اليمين = dialectical inquiry. وB نفس المربعات بس بترتيب غلط — Present يجي أول. وD يخلط العمودين: Debate و Combine ما يجون إلا في dialectical inquiry.

5. According to the chapter, diverse decision-making groups are sometimes less prone to groupthink because:
  1. diverse groups always reach agreement faster than similar groups
  2. group members already differ from each other and thus are less subject to pressures for uniformity
  3. diverse groups do not need devil’s advocacy or dialectical inquiry
  4. diverse groups rally around one central manager such as the CEO
الجواب

B. جملة الكتاب حرفياً: group members already differ from each other and thus are less subject to pressures for uniformity. الفخ في A: الكتاب ما قال أسرع، والقرار الجماعي أصلاً يطوّل؛ وكلمة always لحالها تكفي تشكّك فيه. وC: الكتاب يعرض التنوّع كـطريقة ثالثة جنب التقنيتين، مو بديل يلغيهم. وD هذا وصف groupthink نفسه — عكس المطلوب.
