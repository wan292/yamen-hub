Accounting
Say it: uh-KOWN-ting · the whole system, not only the records
IN ONE LINE
Accounting is not "keeping books". It is a full information system. It picks the events that matter, records them, then communicates them to people who must make a decision.
🧠
Three stations: an eye spots the event, a pen writes it, a mouth reports it. Bookkeeping is only the pen.
EXPLANATION

The textbook definition: accounting is an information and measurement system. It does three jobs on the activities of a business.

EXHIBIT 1.1 — Accounting Functions

This is Exhibit 1.1 from the book. If a question shows a picture, it will look like this — three boxes with two arrows between them. Memorize the order and the line under each box.

The order matters: identify → record → communicate. Any answer that reverses the order is wrong.

LOCAL EXAMPLE

A restaurant in Madinah sold 400 riyals of meals today. Choosing this sale as a financial event = identifying. Writing it into the system with the date and the amount = recording. Printing a month-end report that shows the owner the profit = communicating.

EXAM BOX

Accounting is an information and measurement system that identifies, records, and communicates an organization's business activities.

THE TRAP
  • Recordkeeping (also called bookkeeping) is only one part of accounting — the recording part. Accounting is broader. It also includes analysis and interpretation.
  • Any choice that says accounting = recordkeeping is always wrong. This is the biggest trap in the section.
  • bookkeeping and recordkeeping mean the same thing. Do not treat them as different.
🚗
Financial vs Managerial Accounting
The most important split in this whole section — memorize it
IN ONE LINE
Same information — but who receives it? People outside the company, or people inside it? That is the whole difference.
🧠
The company is a car: whoever holds the steering wheel is internal (managerial). Everyone else, even employees, is external (financial).
EXPLANATION

Accounting is called the language of business, because it carries data that helps people decide. To classify a user, ask one question: does this person run the organization? The users split into two groups.

 External usersInternal users
Who they are They do not run the organization, and their access to the information is limited They run the organization directly
The branchFinancial accountingManagerial accounting
The reports General-purpose financial statements Reports designed for their unique needs

External users: Lenders (creditors) · Shareholders (investors) · External (independent) auditors · Nonmanagerial employees and labor unions · Regulators (such as the IRS) · Voters and government officials · Contributors · Suppliers · Customers

Internal users: Managers of Purchasing · Human resource · Production · Distribution · Marketing · Service · Research and development

LOCAL EXAMPLE

A company in Madinah wants a loan from the bank. The bank is a lender / creditor, so it is an external user. It reads the financial statements to see if the company can repay the money. Inside the same company, the purchasing manager wants a report that tells him what to buy and when. He is an internal user, and the report is built for him alone.

EXAM BOX

Financial accounting focuses on the needs of external users. External users do not directly run the organization and have limited access to its accounting information; they get information from general-purpose financial statements.

Managerial accounting focuses on the needs of internal users. Internal users directly manage the organization, and internal reports are designed for their unique needs.

THE TRAP
  • Do not swap them. Financial = external, Managerial = internal. Link it like this: a manager works inside the company → managerial = internal.
  • External auditors are external users, even though they work on the company. They are independent, so they do not run it.
  • Nonmanagerial employees are external, even though they are employees! They do not run the organization. This trap comes back again and again.
  • Lenders = creditors. Shareholders = investors. The book uses each pair for the same people.

Opportunities in Accounting
Where accountants actually work
🧠
Jobs are a pizza cut in three: the biggest slice, over half, is Private accounting. Public is just a quarter.
EXPLANATION

There are four areas of opportunities: financial · managerial · taxation · accounting-related.

EXHIBIT 1.2 — Accounting Opportunities

Four branches from one box. If a question gives you a job and asks which area it belongs to, this is your map. Cost accounting and Budgeting sit under Managerial — that is the pair students mix up most.

Now look at how the jobs are really split:

EXHIBIT 1.3 — Accounting Jobs by Area

Private = 54% is the biggest share. If the question shows the pie with no numbers, remember that the largest slice is private accounting.

AreaPercentWhat it means

Public accounting24%Auditing, tax, and consulting services sold to clients

The four largest accounting firms: EY · KPMG · PwC · Deloitte

THE TRAP
  • Private accounting is the largest (54%), not public accounting. The question comes in this exact form very often.
  • Public accounting does not mean "government". It means selling accounting services to outside clients.
🩺
Data Analytics
Four types — two look back, two look forward
🧠
A doctor describes the symptoms, diagnoses the cause, predicts what comes next, then prescribes a plan — same four words.
EXPLANATION

Data analytics is the process of analyzing data to find meaningful relationships and trends. There are four types, and they are ordered by difficulty and by value.

TypeIt answersTime
Descriptive Summarizes and describes what happened Past
Diagnostic Reveals the causes of the events Past
Predictive Predicts events that may occur Future
Prescriptive Sets action plans to reach a target Future
EXHIBIT 1.4 — Types of Data Analytics

The four are in order. As you move to the right, both the difficulty and the value go up. The two on the left are about the past. The two on the right are about the future. This order itself is a repeated exam question.

A fifth type is rising: cognitive analytics. It uses AI and machine learning to mimic human thinking.

Data visualization is a graphical display of data that makes its meaning clear. The best-known tool is Tableau. A dashboard is a visual display that puts charts and organized tables together to show trends.

THE TRAP
  • Past: Descriptive + Diagnostic. Future: Predictive + Prescriptive. This is the most common question here.
  • Descriptive vs Diagnostic: the first says what happened, the second says why it happened.
  • Predictive vs Prescriptive: the first predicts, the second recommends a plan.

Test yourself

Read the question, answer it in your head, then open the answer.

1. Which statement best describes the relationship between accounting and recordkeeping?
  1. Accounting and recordkeeping are the same thing.
  2. Recordkeeping is one part of accounting, which also includes analysis and interpretation.
  3. Recordkeeping includes accounting as one of its functions.
  4. Accounting applies only to external users; recordkeeping applies to internal users.
Answer

B. Recordkeeping (or bookkeeping) only writes down the transactions — one part of the job. Accounting is wider, because it also includes analysis and interpretation. Choice A is the most famous trap in this section.

2. A labor union negotiating wages requests the company's financial statements. The union is best classified as:
  1. An internal user, because its members work for the company
  2. An internal user, because it affects payroll decisions
  3. An external user, because it does not directly run the organization
  4. Neither an internal nor an external user
Answer

C. The test is not "do they sit inside the company?" The test is: do they run the organization? A labor union and nonmanagerial employees do not run it, so they are external users. Choices A and B both use that same misunderstanding.

3. A production manager reviews a report on costs and quality to decide whether to change a supplier. This report is an example of:
  1. Financial accounting, because it uses cost data
  2. Managerial accounting, because it serves an internal user
  3. Financial accounting, because it may affect shareholders
  4. Public accounting, because suppliers are external parties
Answer

B. A production manager runs the organization → internal user → managerial accounting. Do not be fooled by the words suppliers or shareholders. What matters is who uses the report, not who is named inside it.

4. An analysis that examines last quarter's sales decline to determine why it occurred is best described as:
  1. Descriptive analytics
  2. Diagnostic analytics
  3. Predictive analytics
  4. Prescriptive analytics
Answer

B. The word why is the key. The question describes a sales decline that already happened. To determine the type, look at the question the analysis answers: Descriptive says what happened, Diagnostic says why it happened. Both are about the past, so choices C and D are in the wrong time frame.

5. According to Exhibit 1.3, the largest share of accounting jobs is in:
  1. Public accounting
  2. Private accounting
  3. Government and not-for-profit
  4. Taxation
Answer

B. Private accounting = 54%, then public accounting = 24%, then government and not-for-profit = 22%. Many students choose public because the name is more famous — that is exactly the trap.
