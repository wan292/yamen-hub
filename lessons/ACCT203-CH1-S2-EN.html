Chapter 1 — Fundamentals of Accounting

ACCT 203 · Chapter 1 · Section 2 of 4: Fundamentals of Accounting
Learning objective C2: Describe the importance of ethics and GAAP.

What this section covers

Three ideas that work together: ethics (why accounting numbers must be reliable), fraud (why people cheat and how companies stop them), and accounting standards (GAAP and IFRS).

The link between them is simple. Numbers are useful only when people trust them. Trust needs three things: good ethics, clear rules, and control.

This is the English twin of your Arabic lesson. The accounting terms stay in English on purpose — that is what your exam paper uses. Tap or hover any dotted word to see the Arabic meaning.
Ethics
say it: ETH-iks
ONE-LINE IDEA
Accounting information has value only when people trust it. That trust starts with the ethics of the person who wrote the numbers.
EXPLANATION

Ethics are beliefs that separate right from wrong. They are accepted standards of good and bad behavior.

An accountant faces ethical choices while preparing financial reports. Those choices affect employee pay and bonuses. They affect whether a product succeeds. A bad choice can hurt the company and the people who work in it.

The textbook repeats an old line: Good ethics are good business.

EXHIBIT 1.5 — Ethical Decision Making

Three steps, always in this order. First you identify the ethical concern. Then you analyze the options and think about all the consequences. Then you decide. If a question gives you a story and asks «what comes next?», go back to this order: identify → analyze → decide.

EXAM BOX

Ethics are beliefs that separate right from wrong. They are accepted standards of good and bad behavior. Ethical decision making follows three steps: identify ethical concerns, analyze options, and make the ethical decision.

The Fraud Triangle
three sides — memorize all three
ONE-LINE IDEA
A person does not commit fraud for one reason. Three factors must come together.
The Fraud Triangle

The three sides are Opportunity · Pressure · Rationalization. If a question gives you two sides and asks for the third, or asks «how many factors?», the answer is three.

EXPLANATION
SideWhat it means
Opportunity The person can cheat, and the risk of getting caught is low.
Pressure Money problems or personal problems push the person to cheat.
Rationalization The person makes an excuse for the act, or does not see it as a crime at all. This side is also called attitude.

The key to stopping fraud is prevention. Prevention is cheaper and more effective than detection.

EXPLANATION

To stop fraud, companies build internal controls. Internal controls are procedures that protect assets, make the accounting reliable, promote efficiency, and uphold company policies.

Examples: good records · physical controls such as locks · independent reviews of the records.

Auditors verify the effectiveness of these internal controls.

EXAM BOX

The fraud triangle shows three factors that push a person to commit fraud: opportunity (low risk of getting caught), pressure, and rationalization. The key to stopping fraud is prevention — it is less expensive and more effective than detection. Internal controls are procedures to protect assets, ensure reliable accounting, promote efficiency, and uphold company policies. Auditors verify the effectiveness of internal controls.

TRAP
  • The sides are three, not two and not four. The answer choices like to drop one.
  • Prevention is cheaper than detection. Any choice that says the opposite is wrong.
  • Internal controls are not an audit. Controls are procedures inside the company; the auditor checks them.
  • Do not mix Opportunity with Pressure. Opportunity = the person can. Pressure = the person needs.
GAAP · FASB · SEC
most multiple-choice questions come from here
ONE-LINE IDEA
If every company used its own method, nobody could compare them. So we need one set of rules, one body to write them, and one body to enforce them.
EXPLANATION

Financial accounting is governed by concepts and rules called generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP).

GAAP wants accounting information to have two qualities:

  • Relevance — the information can change the decision of the user.
  • Faithful representation — the information reflects the real results of the business.

EXPLANATION — INTERNATIONAL STANDARDS

The global economy needs comparability between financial reports. So the IASB (International Accounting Standards Board) issues IFRS (International Financial Reporting Standards).

IFRS is similar to U.S. GAAP, but sometimes different. The FASB and the IASB work together to reduce the differences.

Useful for you in Saudi Arabia: Saudi Arabia applies IFRS through SOCPA (the Saudi Organization for Chartered and Professional Accountants), not U.S. GAAP. But your textbook is American, and your exam comes from the textbook. So study GAAP exactly as the book teaches it, and keep in mind that real Saudi practice runs on IFRS.
EXAM BOX

Financial accounting is governed by concepts and rules known as generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP). GAAP wants information to have relevance and faithful representation. The Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB) is given the task of setting GAAP from the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), a U.S. government agency that oversees proper use of GAAP by companies that sell stock and debt to the public. An audit examines whether financial statements are prepared using GAAP. The International Accounting Standards Board (IASB) issues International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), which are similar to, but sometimes different from, U.S. GAAP.

TRAP
  • FASB sets GAAP; the SEC gives the authority. Do not reverse them — this is the most common question here.
  • The SEC is a government agency. The FASB is not a government agency.
  • IFRS is issued by the IASB, not by the FASB.
  • Relevance = it changes the decision. Faithful representation = it shows the reality accurately. The difference between them is a repeat question.
The Conceptual Framework
four parts, top to bottom
EXPLANATION

The FASB conceptual framework consists of four parts:

PartWhat it does
Objectives To provide useful information to investors, creditors, and others
Qualitative characteristics To require information that has relevance and faithful representation
Elements To define the items that appear in the financial statements
Recognition and measurement To set the rules for when an item is recorded and how it is measured
EXHIBIT 1.6 — Conceptual Framework

The order from top to bottom: Objectives, then Qualitative characteristics, then Elements and Recognition and measurement.

Test yourself

Same English as the exam. Answer first, then open the solution.

1. Which body is given the task of setting GAAP, and from whom does it receive that authority?
  1. The SEC, with authority from the FASB
  2. The FASB, with authority from the SEC
  3. The IASB, with authority from the FASB
  4. The FASB, with authority from the IASB
Answer

B. The FASB is the body that sets GAAP. Its authority comes from the SEC, the U.S. government agency. Choice A reverses the two, and that is the trap the question is built on.

2. An employee with large personal debts notices that no one reviews the petty cash records, and tells himself the company "owes him" for unpaid overtime. Which elements of the fraud triangle are present?
  1. Opportunity only
  2. Pressure and rationalization only
  3. Opportunity, pressure, and rationalization
  4. Pressure only
Answer

C. The debts are pressure. Nobody reviews the records, so there is opportunity. "The company owes me" is rationalization. All three sides are present.

3. Information that accurately reflects the results of a business is said to have:
  1. Relevance
  2. Faithful representation
  3. Comparability
  4. Materiality
Answer

B. Faithful representation means the information shows reality accurately. Relevance is different: it means the information can change the user's decision. That difference is the whole question.

4. Which statement about IFRS is correct?
  1. IFRS is issued by the FASB and is identical to U.S. GAAP
  2. IFRS is issued by the IASB and is identical to U.S. GAAP
  3. IFRS is issued by the IASB and is similar to, but sometimes different from, U.S. GAAP
  4. IFRS is issued by the SEC and replaces U.S. GAAP
Answer

C. The IASB issues IFRS, and IFRS is similar to U.S. GAAP but sometimes different. The word identical is always wrong here.

5. Locks on a warehouse door and independent review of records are examples of:
  1. An audit
  2. Internal controls
  3. GAAP
  4. The conceptual framework
Answer

B. These are internal controls: procedures inside the company that protect assets. An audit is something else. It is an outside examination that checks whether the statements were prepared using GAAP.
