Chapter 1 — Financial Statements

ACCT 203 · Chapter 1 · Section 4 of 4: Financial Statements
Objective P2: Identify and prepare basic financial statements and explain how they interrelate.

End of the chapter — everything comes together here

There are four statements. They are prepared in a fixed order. Each one feeds the next one. The exam question is not "what are the statements". The exam asks which one covers a period and which one covers a point in time, and how they link to each other.

The Four Financial Statements
The order is deliberate — do not change it

EXAM BOX

The income statement, the statement of retained earnings, and the statement of cash flows are prepared for a period of time. The balance sheet is prepared as of a point in time.

A statement's heading identifies the company, the statement title, and the date or time period.

TRAP
  • Three cover a period. One covers a point. Only the balance sheet is at a point in time. This is the most common question in this section.
  • This is why the heading of an income statement says For Month Ended December 31, while the balance sheet says only December 31. The heading itself reveals the answer.
How the Statements Interrelate
Half of objective P2 is exactly this
1 Net income from the income statement is used to compute retained earnings.
2 Retained earnings is used to prepare the balance sheet.
3 Cash from the balance sheet is used to reconcile the statement of cash flows.

This is why the order is mandatory. You cannot prepare the statement of retained earnings before you know net income. You cannot prepare the balance sheet before you know retained earnings.

EXAM BOX

Net income is used to compute retained earnings. Retained earnings is used to prepare the balance sheet. Cash from the balance sheet is used to reconcile the statement of cash flows.

Income Statement
Revenues first, expenses second
EXPLANATION

Revenues come first. Expenses come after them. Expenses are the costs used to generate the reported revenues.

  • Net income — this occurs when revenues exceed expenses.
  • Net loss — this occurs when expenses exceed revenues.
  • Net income (or net loss) appears at the bottom of the statement.
  • It is sometimes called earnings or profit. Same thing.

⚠ Shareholder investments and dividends are not part of income.

Statement of Retained Earnings
This is where dividends belong
EXPLANATION

Retained earnings = cumulative net income minus any dividend distributions.

The statement shows four things: the beginning balance · what increases it (net income) · what decreases it (dividends and net loss) · and the ending balance.

The ending balance is carried over and shown on the balance sheet. That is link number 2 above.

The beginning balance: for FastForward it is zero, because the company did not exist before December 1. An existing company starts with the ending balance of the prior period, for example November 30.

EXAM BOX

Retained earnings is cumulative net income minus any dividend distributions. The statement shows beginning retained earnings, events that increase it (net income), and events that decrease it (dividends and net loss). Ending retained earnings is carried over and reported on the balance sheet.

TRAP
  • Dividends are subtracted here, not on the income statement. If you subtract them from revenues, your net income will be wrong.
  • A new company has a beginning balance of zero. An existing company starts with the ending balance of the prior period.
  • A net loss decreases retained earnings, just like dividends do.
Statement of Cash Flows
Three sections, one total
EXPLANATION

There are three sections: Operating · Investing · Financing. Their total is the change in cash.

Then you add the beginning cash balance. The result is the ending cash balance. That number is the same cash number on the balance sheet. That is link number 3.

Test yourself

1. Which financial statement is prepared as of a point in time rather than for a period of time?
  1. Income statement
  2. Statement of retained earnings
  3. Balance sheet
  4. Statement of cash flows
Answer

C. Only the balance sheet is at a point in time. The other three are for a period of time. This is a guaranteed mark if you memorize it.

2. A company reports revenues of $9,000 and expenses of $11,500. It also paid $500 of dividends. Net income (loss) is:
  1. Net income of $2,500
  2. Net loss of $2,500
  3. Net loss of $3,000
  4. Net income of $3,000
Answer

B. 9,000 − 11,500 = (2,500) — a loss. Dividends never enter the calculation of net income. Their place is the statement of retained earnings. Choice C is the trap, because it also subtracts the $500.

3. Beginning retained earnings is $12,000. Net income is $5,000 and dividends are $2,000. Ending retained earnings is:
  1. $19,000
  2. $15,000
  3. $9,000
  4. $5,000
Answer

B. 12,000 + 5,000 − 2,000 = 15,000. Add net income, then subtract dividends — in that order.

4. In what order must the statements be prepared?
  1. Balance sheet → income statement → retained earnings → cash flows
  2. Income statement → retained earnings → balance sheet → cash flows
  3. Cash flows → balance sheet → income statement → retained earnings
  4. Any order; they are independent
Answer

B. Net income feeds retained earnings. Retained earnings feeds the balance sheet. Cash on the balance sheet reconciles the statement of cash flows. Choice D is completely wrong, because the statements interrelate.

5. A shareholder invests $20,000 cash in the company. On the income statement this is reported as:
  1. Revenue of $20,000
  2. An expense of $20,000
  3. Net income of $20,000
  4. It is not reported on the income statement
Answer

D. Shareholder investments and dividends are not part of income. The investment appears in Common Stock and in the financing activities section — not on the income statement.
